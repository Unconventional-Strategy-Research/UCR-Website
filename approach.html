Our Approach

How we think
about thinking

USR is built on three principles. Not a methodology — a set of commitments about how to do this work honestly.

Principles

What we stand for

01
Research First
Study before you build
Original frameworks don't come from nowhere. Before USR develops its own models, we spend serious time with the ones that already exist — the problem-solving processes, portfolio thinking, and results-delivery approaches developed by the world's leading strategy firms. Understanding what the best have built is not a detour. It's the foundation.
02
Learn in Public
The process is the product
We don't wait until we're finished to share what we've found. The synthesis work, the questions we can't yet answer, the tools we build as tests — all of it gets published as it develops. Transparency isn't a risk. It's the point. A community learns faster when the work is visible.
03
Build to Understand
Tools as proof of comprehension
The best test of whether you've internalized a framework is whether you can build something from it. CaseIQ wasn't built to be a product first — it was built because turning six consulting frameworks into a 75-question drill forced us to understand them at a different depth. Every Lab tool starts the same way.
Method

How the research works

Step 01
Identify a framework or model
Starting with the canonical frameworks from the world's top strategy firms — issue trees, profitability structures, portfolio matrices, MECE logic, and beyond.
Step 02
Break it apart
What is the core logic? What does it assume? Where does it work well and where does it fail? What problems is it actually solving?
Step 03
Synthesize and publish
Write up the synthesis — what we found, what surprised us, how it connects to other models. Published as it's done, not when it's perfect.
Step 04
Build a tool from it
If the framework is trainable, we build something that trains it. The Lab is the output of this step — interactive tools that operationalize what we've learned.
Why the top firms first
The world's leading strategy firms represent the highest-density concentration of structured thinking methodology available publicly. They're not the only firms that matter — but they're the right starting point before developing original models.
What comes after
Eventually, USR develops its own frameworks — particularly for community-scale strategy and ecosystem thinking, contexts the big firms don't serve well. That work starts from the foundation being built now.
Who this is for
People who want to think more rigorously — whether that's for case interviews, career decisions, community leadership, or just the satisfaction of understanding how good strategy actually works.

Direction

Where this is going

USR is early. The research is ongoing, the Lab has one tool, and the original frameworks don't exist yet.

That's intentional. We're not presenting a finished institution. We're building one in public — and the people who find this early are the ones who'll shape what it becomes.

The goal is a practice that produces original strategic thinking for contexts the major firms don't prioritise: communities, ecosystems, non-traditional organizations. Getting there requires doing the foundational work first.

That's what's happening now.
